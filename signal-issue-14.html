The week in signals

This week the machines started showing their work — and the work started showing its cost. Four stories, one direction.

Robots that learn by watching. NVIDIA's GR00T 2 learns from 100,000 hours of open human video, and the numbers are the story: 40 out of 50 tasks done zero-shot, 71% with one demonstration, 95% with five. The idea this episode tests: watching is becoming the cheapest way to teach a robot anything. (Futurenomics, Nov 10.)

Thirty-five seconds to stop a runaway AI. OpenAI's Control Room can halt a runaway research run in 35 seconds, fail-closed, with job IDs and approved safety cases. The scope is research inference only — not ChatGPT, not the API — but the direction is clear: the labs are building brakes as fast as they build engines. (Futurenomics, Nov 11.)

Six million lines of proof. DeepMind's AlphaProof Code machine-checked the formal verification of Signal's encryption — libsignal and SPQR, down to the machine code, 6.5 million lines. The idea this episode tests: formal proof turns trust in software from an expensive expert service into a cheap, machine-checked commodity. (Futurenomics, Nov 12.)

A hundred and forty-five billion parameters on your laptop. Edge0's open-weight agent machine runs a 145B model on one laptop GPU — 29GB peak memory, 80+ tokens a second. The idea this episode tests: when a frontier-grade model runs on your own laptop, the cloud's rent on intelligence falls to zero. (Futurenomics, Nov 13.)

One thread through all four: the proof is getting cheaper, the machines are getting closer, and the control is getting faster. Build like the capability keeps compounding — and keep the kill switch in your own hand.
