World Models

We are awake 16 hours a day and we will spend 8 hours playing and working in these worlds.

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4 yesterday. You describe a place and it builds one you can walk through, in real time, with its own sound. Weather, music, people who talk back. Right now it's a research preview for trusted testers. It's "coming soon" to Google AI Ultra subscribers through Project Genie, and Ultra starts at $99.99 a month. So call it a $100 ticket, once the door opens.

A few days earlier Tencent put Hy-Nomas on Hugging Face. 8B parameters. Apache 2.0. Weights and training code, all of it. It runs on one RTX 4090 if you squeeze it.

Honestly, you can't compare the two. Genie 4 is in another league. But here's the part I keep thinking about. Google's Pro plan is $19.99. If Qwen or Kimi shipped a Genie 3-class world model for $20 a month, that $100 wall gets thin fast. It wouldn't need to be better than Google. Just good enough, and cheap.

Where does this go? My read, and it is a read, not a forecast with a footnote: within about two years, most customer service and a lot of everyday interactions could happen inside world models. You won't open a chat window. You'll step into a room. Maybe through AR glasses like the Oakley Meta line (today those are camera, audio and Meta AI, no screen; Meta's display model is the Ray-Ban Display). Maybe one day through a Neuralink-style brain interface. Either way, the world around you gets more context. More layers. Your brain already throws away most of what's in front of you. These models could hand some of it back.

Now the math.

In August an unreleased Claude raised a long-standing bound on the zeros of the Riemann zeta function, from 41.6% to 67.2%. Anthropic published it as Claude's paper, with its own mathematicians as validators. That kicked off a real argument about whether a model can be an author. A month later OpenAI said an internal model found a blow-up in the Navier-Stokes equations, and that turned into a credit fight too. OpenAI's own message to the two mathematicians working the same problem said the academic accolades should go to them, the humans. Who signs the proof is now a live question.

Here's my view: these proofs won't stay on paper. They'll show up as cheaper materials, better simulations, and factories that run with the lights off, built back home through automation and robotics. That's how this tech gets cheap enough for everyone.

The schools are already moving. A September summit of mathematicians at Harvard told PhD programs to add training in AI tools and formal proof, and to stop grading mainly on written work. Math and physics are moving faster than the people who teach them expected.

And the money. Anthropic asked its own staff how fast AI would move. Their median answers, run through Anthropic's economic model, imply 17.1% US GDP growth by 2030. The median American's answers imply 5.4%. That kind of growth could cut poverty hard. But the same model's most extreme scenario also has a lot more people out of work in knowledge jobs.

So the next 18 to 24 months could be rough. How rough depends on how fast we stop arguing with the phase transition and start building for it. It's coming either way.
