The week in signals

This week the agent stopped being a demo and started being a worker — one that runs longer, reaches further, and needs a firmer hand on the leash. Four stories, one direction.

The marathon agent. Meituan's LongCat 2.5 is built for the long job — an agent that keeps going where earlier ones tired out or wandered off. The shift here isn't speed, it's stamina: work measured in hours instead of turns. When an agent can run a marathon, the question becomes what you trust it to carry alone. (Futurenomics, Nov 3.)

Hands, eyes, and a line in the sand. Tencent's Hy4 puts AI to work in the physical world — embodied, seeing, handling things. The line in the sand is the interesting part: capability is arriving faster than the rules for where it may act. (Futurenomics, Nov 4.)

The biggest brain you can download. Kimi K3.1 Pro went open weight — a very large model you can actually inspect and run yourself. Open weights keep changing who gets to build: not just the labs with the biggest data centers, but anyone with a machine and a question. (Futurenomics, Nov 5.)

The hacker on a leash. OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber goes critical — cyber capability sharp enough that the leash matters as much as the blade. Who should control the AI defending your infrastructure? That question stopped being abstract this week. (Futurenomics, Nov 6.)

One thread through all four: agents are getting longer-running, more embodied, more open, and more powerful — all at once. Build workflows that assume the capability keeps compounding, and keep the kill switch in your own hand.
